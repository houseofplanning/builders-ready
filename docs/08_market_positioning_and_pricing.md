## 0. The one-paragraph version

There is no UK-native premium-residential client-portal SaaS that competes head-on with what Builders Ready will be. The closest functional products — Buildertrend and BuildBook — are US-built, USD-billed, seat-priced, and US-flavoured (lien terminology, US tax). The closest UK products — Powered Now, Tradify, Yourtradebase, Joblogic — sit a category lower (tradesperson invoicing / FSM), not premium-residential project narrative. The one UK product in the same architectural space — Housebuilder Pro — targets volume housebuilders at ~£490/user/month, a different buyer entirely. **Your working numbers (£24.99 / £49.99 / £99.99) are aggressive at the low end and likely under-priced at the top.** I recommend launching at **£29 / £69 / £149** — still meaningfully cheaper than every direct competitor, with the £149 tier reading as "a real product" rather than "too cheap to trust."

## 2. What the table actually tells us

Three patterns emerge once you stare at the numbers:

**A. The "real client portal" tier in 2026 starts at ~£54-£62/mo and tops out around £200-£270/mo for SMB plans.** Houzz Pro (£54), BuildBook Solo (£62), Buildxact (£113), Buildertrend Essential (£267). Below £54 you don't get a proper homeowner portal — you get a quoting tool or a job-card view. Above £270 you're paying for enterprise GC features your buyer doesn't need.

**B. UK-built tooling clusters either far below the portal tier or far above it.** Powered Now / Tradify / Yourtradebase / Joblogic sit at £15-£45/user/mo and explicitly **don't** offer a real client-facing project narrative. Housebuilder Pro sits at ~£490/user/mo and is built for developers, not boutique builders. The middle is empty in the UK.

**C. Pricing is overwhelmingly per-user-seat. Builders Ready is per-active-project.** That's a genuine differentiator. A 10-person UK builder using BuildBook pays for 10 seats and gets unlimited projects; using Builders Ready they pay for one subscription tied to the count of live jobs. For the typical buyer (4-8 staff, 5-15 concurrent projects) per-project billing is **cheaper than per-seat once the team is larger than three.**

The opportunity is therefore: **"the UK-native client portal for premium-residential builders, priced sensibly by project."** No one currently owns that sentence.

## 3. Pricing recommendation

Two ways to play this. I'd back option A.

### Option A — Confident-but-affordable (recommended)

| Tier | Active projects | Monthly | Annual (£/mo equiv.) | Annual total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Starter** | up to 10 | **£29** | **£23** | £276/yr |
| **Pro** | up to 50 | **£69** | **£55** | £660/yr |
| **Unlimited** | unlimited | **£149** | **£119** | £1,428/yr |

Why these numbers:

- **Starter at £29:** Sits just below YourTradebase (£29 single-user) and well below Powered Now Premium (£37). Still half what BuildBook charges and a fifth of Buildertrend. The £29 number reads as "less than £1/day" — psychologically below the £30 procurement-discretion threshold for almost every UK SMB.
- **Pro at £69:** Lands between Joblogic Standard (£45/user) and Houzz Pro UK Starter (£54) but offers a category that neither does. Cheaper than BuildBook Pro (~£117 annual / $149) and Buildxact entry (~£113). A 5-PM builder paying £69 vs £225 (3 × Joblogic seats) saves £156/mo — that's the buying conversation.
- **Unlimited at £149:** Far below BuildBook Business (~£196), Houzz Pro Pro (~£196+), Buildxact mid-tier (~£250+), and Buildertrend everything (£267+). High enough to read as a real product for a £15M-revenue builder; low enough to win on price every time.

### Option B — Your original working numbers

£24.99 / £49.99 / £99.99. Still defensible, still wins on price. Risks:

- **£99.99 at the top is the weak point.** A builder running £10M+ of projects looking at a "unlimited" tier at £100/mo will assume the product is unserious or has a catch. We'd lose buyers who'd happily pay £150 because £100 looked too cheap to trust. Worth pulling closer to £129-£179.
- **£24.99 at entry has a smaller signal-of-quality cost.** It's near-impulse pricing — good for adoption, slightly self-defeating against Houzz Pro (£54). But "Starter" tiers are supposed to be on-ramps; cheapness is fine here.

### Option C — Premium signalling (not recommended for v1)

£39 / £89 / £199. Closes the gap to Houzz Pro / BuildBook, signals confidence. Risks under-attracting the price-sensitive long tail of UK independent builders. I'd file this as a 12-month repricing option once you have proof-points (case studies, builder logos on the marketing site).

## 4. Why this beats the alternatives, line-by-line

What a UK premium-residential builder gets from Builders Ready Pro (£69/mo) vs. the comparable competitor purchases:

- **vs. Buildertrend Essential (£267/mo):** 1/4 the price, UK-native invoicing with bank-transfer (no US lien language), £ billing, GDPR-aligned data residency, a homeowner app that doesn't feel transplanted from Texas.
- **vs. BuildBook Solo (£62/mo):** Cheaper at single-user level *if* the builder has any team, because BuildBook charges per seat. Equivalent client UX. £ vs $ billing.
- **vs. Houzz Pro Starter (£54/mo):** Marginally cheaper, but more importantly built for builders not designers, with explicit construction-stage timelines vs. Houzz's design-led workflow.
- **vs. Joblogic Standard (£45/user × 3 PMs = £135/mo):** Half the cost at the same team size, and the *clients* get a real app — Joblogic's customer login is utility-grade FSM.
- **vs. Housebuilder Pro (~£490/user/mo):** 1/7 the price for a similar feature surface, sized for boutique builders rather than volume housebuilders.

The pitch line for the marketing page essentially writes itself: **"The UK's client portal for premium-residential builders. From £29/month. Built in £, not $."**

## 5. Tactical pricing notes

These are small calls that matter when you actually publish the page.

- **Round numbers, not .99.** £29 reads as confident; £29.99 reads as supermarket. Premium-residential builders are not Aldi shoppers. (Recommendation overrides the working numbers' .99 convention.)
- **Quote excl. VAT** with a small "+VAT" indicator. UK B2B SaaS convention; builders are VAT-registered and reclaim input VAT.
- **20% annual saving** keeps muscle memory simple. The recommended numbers shown above use 20%.
- **No "founder" or "early-bird" discount on the public page.** Lock annual at 20% off, then offer 50% off the first three months as a one-off coupon code via direct outreach to your first ~20 builders. That gets the wedge without anchoring the public price low.
- **No free tier.** A free tier on a B2B SaaS this targeted attracts time-wasters and devalues the brand. 14-day trial with card upfront is the right wedge.

## 6. Two pricing-adjacent design calls worth surfacing now

These came out of the competitor scan and are easier to decide before the marketing page is built.

**Annual-only at Unlimited?** BuildBook offers a meaningful discount only on annual at the top tier. If we offer monthly Unlimited, we attract churn. A builder doing £15M doesn't pay £149 month-to-month — they want an invoice they can put through procurement. **Recommendation:** offer both monthly and annual at all three tiers in v1, but quietly nudge Unlimited buyers to annual via the signup flow (default toggle to "Annual"). Re-evaluate at 6 months.

**Trial length at Unlimited?** A builder considering Unlimited probably won't validate it in 14 days — they're integrating it into their operating model. **Recommendation:** keep 14 days for Starter and Pro, but extend to 30 days for Unlimited on request (manual override, not a public option). Avoids gaming.
